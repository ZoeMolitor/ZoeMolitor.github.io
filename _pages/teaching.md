**I am teaching Structural Geology at Boston College for Fall 2026.**

I design my courses with flexibility in mind, first focusing on learning goals like improving 3D spatial reasoning, knowledge and application of geologic jargon, and ability to critically analyze observations and interpretations from your own work as well as others'.

I teach through a dynamic, field-focused and/or hands-on classroom. I build student-centered choices into my assignments and course to increase engagement and excitement from my students. Additionally, I integrate visual and hands-on demonstrations, models, and interactive codes and computer apps into the lecture time of all my courses to allow students to practice applying terms and concepts directly to real-world rocks and data. Some examples of this include small sandbox models to display the development of thin- and thick-skinned orogenic belts, 3D physical and digital models of geologic structures to better visualize the 3D nature of geology, and plug and play codes and webapps where students may enter simple geologic data for plotting or more complex data and parameters to run 2D models of plate boundary or structure development (e.g., faults/shear zones, folds, metamorphic field gradients). And most importantly, I emphasize field-based learning. Geology is a fundamentally hands-on and visual science, and field work is the best way to reinforce terms, concepts, and critical thinking skills in students of all experience levels. The tools that I use as a geologist a best applied in a field setting or with real data.
